Absorption is largest at critical coupling, β = 1, where the two ports together leak as much energy as the walls absorb (Qext = 2Qint). Half the power is then absorbed, a quarter transmitted and a quarter reflected. Strong overcoupling gives near-full transmission but a broad line; strong undercoupling gives a sharp line you can barely see.

The model behind the curves

A single cavity mode coupled symmetrically to an input and an output line, each with external quality factor Qext:

S21(f) = 2 / Qext1 / QL + 2j (f − f0) / f0,    1QL = 1Qint + 2Qext
  • The peak height is |S21(f0)| = 2QL/Qext = β/(1 + β), with the total coupling β = 2Qint/Qext. It approaches 1 (0 dB) only when the ports dominate the losses.
  • The full width at half power is Δf = f0/QL, set by the total loss rate, internal plus external.
  • The phase swings by 180° across the resonance, passing through 0° at f0 and ±45° at the half-power points.
  • In the complex plane, S21 traces a circle through the origin with diameter 2QL/Qext. A real measurement adds cable delay, attenuation and impedance mismatch, which rotate, scale and distort this circle.
  • Sign convention: the phase falls with frequency (the e+jωt engineering convention). Some texts use the opposite sign.

The transmission-line cavity

The second tab computes S21 exactly. The middle section is a line of impedance Zin, propagation constant γ = α + jβ and length L, between two lines of impedance Zout. With r = Zin/Zout:

S21 = 22 cosh γL + (r + 1/r) sinh γL
  • Each step reflects Γ = (Zout − Zin)/(Zout + Zin). A sharp resonance needs a large mismatch: a ratio of 0.2 or 5 gives only |Γ| ≈ 0.67.
  • Resonances sit where the section is a whole number of half wavelengths long, fn = n c/(2L). The step reflections add 0 or 2π to the round trip, so the impedance ratio does not move them in this ideal model. Real steps carry fringing fields that shift them slightly.
  • The wave loses 1 − |Γ|² of its power through each port on every bounce, so the energy decays as |Γ|4 per round trip. That gives Qext = nπ / (−ln|Γ|) for each port: only the impedance ratio and the mode number set it.
  • Loss in the line gives Qint = β/(2α). With conductor loss growing as √f, a shorter cavity resonates higher and has a higher Qint, in proportion to √f0.
  • With “Loss depends on Zin” ticked, the coax keeps its outer diameter, so changing Zin changes the inner conductor. Conductor loss then scales as (1 + b/a)/ln(b/a), lowest near 77 Ω. A very thin inner wire (high Zin) or a very fat one (low Zin) costs internal Q.
  • The dotted “equivalent lumped model” is the first tab’s formula fed with these f0, Qint and Qext. It matches near a resonance and fails once QL drops towards a few, where neighbouring modes overlap.
  • “Align phase” multiplies S21 by (−1)n so that it is real and positive at the chosen resonance, as in the lumped model. Without it you see the extra half-wavelengths of propagation phase.
